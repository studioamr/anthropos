LIFEISAMIRACLE.
I · THE MIRACLE

Of all the ways matter could arrange itself, one learned to look at the sky.

Fourteen billion years. One small planet. The atoms of a dead star became a creature that sings, builds, loves and asks why. A human life is the rarest thing we know of in the universe. It was never meant to be spent waiting for the weekend.

II · THE ARRANGEMENT

For two hundred years we lived by the clock.

The steam engine moved us from the fields to the mill. Cities were built around jobs, families scattered to follow wages, and a person became what they did for a living. We still live inside that arrangement.

III · WHAT WE LOST

Somewhere along the way, we forgot what it was all for.

We traded the forum for the commute and the festival for the weekend. We learned to introduce ourselves by our jobs, to measure a life in tasks completed, and to fill what little time was left with screens. We became more productive than any people in history, and less sure of why.

IV · WHY NOW

The work that filled a human life can now be done by machines.

V · WHAT A FREE PERSON DOES

Not leisure. Purpose.

    I · WHAT WE ARE BUILDING

    A CITY FOR
    WHAT COMES AFTER

    Anthropos is a new city for one hundred thousand people, designed from the first stone for a world in which machines do the work. It produces its own energy, water and food. It is coordinated by an intelligence that its citizens own. And it is organized not around jobs, but around the four things a free person does.

    01

    A city that sustains itself

    Sun and wind for power, the sea and the rain for water, greenhouses and fields within sight for food, and a closed loop for waste. The floor of life is paid before anyone wakes up.

    02

    A society of free people

    Founders, artists, scholars, athletes and builders, living side by side, with time to practice, study, compete and create. Not a resort. A culture.

    03

    A republic with a mind

    An intelligence runs the systems and drafts the proposals. Citizens deliberate in person and decide. A short written charter stands above both.

    II · THE FOUR VOCATIONS

    If the machine produces,
    the citizen creates.

    I

    Ars · the arts

    Music, sculpture, film, architecture, dance and the night. The arts district wraps the great park and the lake. Its courtyards hold studios rented at cost, workshops for wood, metal and stone, rehearsal rooms and small theaters. Patronage is paid for by the wealth the machines produce, so an artist never has to sell their time to make their work.

    • Studios at cost
    • The Amphitheater
    • City patronage
    II

    Scientia · knowledge

    Philosophy, science and engineering, studied to understand rather than to be employed. The university climbs the first slope of the hills around a glass library that never closes. Laboratories are open to anyone. Above it, the observatory keeps the sky dark enough to see the stars from every roof.

    • The University
    • Open laboratories
    • The Observatory
    III

    Virtus · honor

    The body as a work. Athletics, fencing, wrestling, swimming, the mountain and the sea. Every district has a gymnasium, the stadium hosts the games and festivals of the city, and the great baths close every day. Compete with honor, lose with dignity, train again.

    • The Stadium
    • Gymnasia
    • The Baths
    IV

    Res Publica · the forum

    The public thing. At the center stands the Capitol, and around it the squares where citizens meet in person to decide what binds them: what to build, what to protect, who to welcome. The Mind prepares the proposals and answers every question. It never decides.

    • The forum
    • The charter

    VI · WHAT IT IS NOT

    A gated resort. It is a city, open to everyone who shares its aims.

    A tax haven or a secession. It lives under the laws of the country that hosts it, and enriches it.

    A city run by a machine. The Mind serves the forum. People decide.

    A fantasy of leisure. It is a life of practice, study, competition and responsibility.
