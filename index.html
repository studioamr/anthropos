01 · The return

The factory took our days. Superintelligence can give them back. We are building the cities where that happens.

For two centuries, life has been organized around a wage: the clock, the commute, the office. Machines can now produce most of what a city needs. Anthropos builds cities that keep that wealth for the people who live in them, so their time goes back to making things, learning, training, raising families and running the city together.

02 · After labor

A human
on earth.

Two hundred years ago we left the fields and the workshops for the factory. We learned to live by the clock, to commute, to call ourselves by our jobs. Superintelligence and abundant energy can end that arrangement. We are building the first cities designed for what comes after it.

1850 · the mill

I

Before the factory

Life was organized around family, faith, the city and the seasons. Work was part of a day, not the whole of a life. People spent their afternoons with family, in the square and in the workshop.

II

The Industrial Revolution

The steam engine moved people from the land to the mill. Time began to belong to the clock. Cities were built around jobs, families scattered to follow wages, and a person became what they did for a living. We still live inside that arrangement.

III

Self-sustaining cities

Machines and superintelligence can now run what the factory needed us for. An Anthropos city makes its own energy from the sun, its own water from the sea, its own food from autonomous farms, and runs its services with intelligent systems. The city works so its citizens don’t have to.

IV

The return

Freed from labor, people return to what matters: making, learning, training, building with others and running their own city. A life measured by excellence, beauty, friendship and the common good. Not leisure. Purpose.

03 · The opportunity

Why now.

The superintelligence economy is about to create more wealth, and more free time, than any economy in history. Almost nobody is building the places where that life will be lived.

AI is ending the wage

Automation has reached knowledge and service work. The question is no longer whether work becomes optional, but where people will live when it does.

Old cities can’t be rewired

Existing cities were built around the factory and the office. It is faster to build a new city around the new economy than to rebuild an old one street by street.

Infrastructure is the business

Energy, water, food and data owned by the city are not a cost. They are recurring revenue that pays for the life of its citizens.

The city is the product

As master developer, Anthropos captures the value created between raw land and a living city, and keeps earning for as long as the city runs.

10 · The raise

Four rounds
to a city.

One billion dollars builds the first city from bare land to a living place. Capital comes in as the risk goes down, through a regulated structure in which each investor’s share of the project company is recorded as a digital token.

$0Target $1,000,000,000
01One company

A project company owns the land, the buildings and the income of the first city.

02Digital shares

Its equity is divided into tokens, issued and registered by a regulated platform after identity checks.

03Money follows milestones

Capital waits in escrow and is released as land, permits, infrastructure and homes are delivered.

04Returns in proportion

Income from homes, hospitality and city services flows back to holders, who can transfer their tokens after the holding period.

11 · Declaration

Declaration of Return
MMXXVI

When the work that once filled a human life can be done by machines, a people may choose what to do with the life that is returned to them. We choose to build.

We hold that a life is not measured by its output; that the city exists for its citizens and not for its economy; and that every generation is free to found the institutions its age makes possible.

For two centuries our cities were organized around the factory and the office. They taught us to live by the clock, to scatter our families after wages, and to name ourselves by our jobs. That order is ending.

We, the founding citizens of Anthropos, declare our intent to build self-sustaining cities: cities that make their own energy, water and food, run their services with intelligent systems, and give their people the time that labor took.

We start from a simple question: what does a day look like once energy, water, food and services are produced by the city itself? We mean to build the places where that day is lived: the square, the workshop, the school, the long table, the sea.

  • To cultivate excellence, beauty and friendship in every part of life.
  • To build cities that sustain themselves and serve their citizens.
  • To keep every binding decision in human hands, under a written charter.
  • To welcome anyone who shares these aims, whatever their origin.
  • To live under the laws of every country that hosts us, and to enrich it.
  • To pass these cities on, better than we found them, to those who come after.

Signed by the founding citizens of Anthropos, in the year two thousand and twenty-six.
